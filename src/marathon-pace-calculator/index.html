Formula and limitations

Pace (seconds per kilometer) = goal finish time (total seconds) ÷ race distance (kilometers).

This gives the steady average pace you need to hold to reach your goal.

  • Grade: Flat-pace targets can be optimistic on uphills and conservative on runnable downhills.
  • Elevation gain: Repeated climbs can make the total effort implied by a flat pace too optimistic, even if the course finishes at its starting elevation.
  • Fatigue: If you slow late in the race, the steady-pace checkpoints can be optimistic.
  • Weather: Heat or a headwind can make the plan optimistic; a helpful tailwind can make it conservative.
  • Running tangents: Wide turns or weaving add distance, so the predicted time at the planned pace can be optimistic.

Displayed pace and splits are rounded to the nearest second. Checkpoints use the unrounded pace.

FAQ

How do I use the marathon split calculator?

Enter your goal finish time and choose a distance. Read the cumulative times at the 5 km and mile checkpoints or download both sets in one CSV.

Can I calculate splits for a shorter race?

Yes. Choose a distance preset or enter a custom distance. Both tables end at the selected finish distance.
