Marathon Cumulative vs. Segment Splits

If cumulative time is 28:30 at 5 km and 57:15 at 10 km, the second 5K takes 28:45, averaging 5:45/km. Subtract 1,710 from 3,435 seconds, then divide by 5.

Turn checkpoint times into segment pace

Why does the overall pace at 10 km differ?

Dividing 3,435 seconds by the full 10 km gives 343.5 seconds per kilometer, or 5:43.50/km. The second segment alone averages 5:45/km. Overall pace combines both segments, while segment pace describes the interval between the two markers.

What if one checkpoint is halfway?

Subtract the distances too. From 20 km to halfway at 21.0975 km, the interval is 1.0975 km. If elapsed time rises from 1:55:00 to 2:01:30, divide the 390-second difference by 1.0975 for 5:55.35/km.

What if the clock bases differ?

Convert both readings to the same starting point before subtracting. If one reading is chip elapsed time and another is a shared gun clock, a start delay can contaminate the segment difference. Use consistent elapsed readings and include stops within the segment.
