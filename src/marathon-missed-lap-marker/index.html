Missed a Marathon Lap Marker? Calculate Your Split

Missed the 21 km lap after recording 20 km? If your next recorded split at 22 km is 11:20, divide 680 seconds by 2 km for 5:40/km. The combined split still gives your average pace over those two kilometers.

Read the distance between the recorded markers

Subtract the last recorded course marker from the current one. From 20 km to 22 km, the interval is two kilometers. Divide total elapsed seconds between those markers by two. This calculation assumes you know the marker where the previous lap began and that the timer includes the full interval.

Can I reconstruct the missed individual kilometer?

A combined 11:20 could be 5:30 plus 5:50, or 5:40 plus 5:40. Both average 5:40/km. Without another timestamp at the intermediate marker, the combined split alone cannot identify each kilometer's time.

What if I missed a mile marker?

Use miles for both markers. A lap from mile 18 to mile 20 taking 18:40 covers two miles, giving 9:20 per mile. Divide 560 seconds per mile by 1.609344 to convert that average to about 5:47.97/km.

How do I compare the combined split with my marathon chart?

Subtract the planned cumulative time at the earlier marker from the planned time at the later marker. At an exact four-hour even pace, two kilometers take 682.55 seconds, or 11:22.55. A measured 11:20 is 2.55 seconds faster across the interval. Rounded chart entries can shift that difference slightly.
