Marathon Pace Chart With a Start Delay

For a 4:00 chip-time marathon, crossing the start 3:00 after the gun puts your halfway clock target at 2:03:00. Add your start delay to the chart's 2:00:00 halfway split. Your chip-time pace stays 5:41.27/km.

Translate your chart to the shared race clock

These calculations assume the course clock starts at the same gun used to measure your start delay. A clock for a different wave needs its own offset. Record the clock reading as you cross the start mat, then add that offset to each chip-time checkpoint.

Am I behind if the halfway clock reads 2:03?

With a three-minute offset, 2:03:00 minus 0:03:00 equals 2:00:00 elapsed since crossing the start. That matches the halfway split for an even four-hour chip-time plan. Compare the same time basis at each marker.

What if my goal uses gun time?

A four-hour gun-time deadline with a three-minute start delay leaves 3:57:00 between the mats. Divide 14,220 seconds by 42.195 km to get 5:37.01/km. An eight-minute delay leaves 3:52:00, requiring 5:29.90/km. Check which timing basis your event uses for your goal, awards or cutoff.

How do I recalculate at 30 km?

If the clock reads 2:55:00 and your offset is 3:00, elapsed chip time is 2:52:00. A four-hour chip goal leaves 1:08:00 for 12.195 km. Divide 4,080 by 12.195 for 5:34.56/km. Use total elapsed time, including stops, when you compare against the finish goal.
