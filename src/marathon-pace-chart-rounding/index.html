Marathon Pace Chart Rounding: Why Rounded Pace Misses Your Finish Time

A 4:00:00 marathon has 14,400 seconds. Divide by 42.195 km and you get approximately 341.272663 seconds per kilometer, displayed as 5:41/km. Hold that displayed pace exactly and 341 × 42.195 = 14,388.495 seconds, or 3:59:48 rounded to the nearest second. That is 11.505 seconds early. Using the unrounded pace, (14,400 ÷ 42.195) × 42.195 = 14,400 seconds, exactly 4:00:00.

How to read the tables

Convert each goal to seconds as hours × 3,600 + minutes × 60 + seconds. Divide by the course distance, round the quotient to the nearest whole second, multiply that rounded pace by the course distance, then subtract the goal seconds. Finish clocks are rounded to the nearest second; signed differences use the unrounded finish and are shown to three decimals. A minus sign means early; a plus sign means late.

Why the mile table differs

Finish error equals (displayed seconds per unit − exact seconds per unit) × course distance. At four hours, 5:41/km finishes 11.505 seconds early, while 9:09/mi finishes 5.902 seconds early. The maximum rounding drift is half a second per unit, or about 21.10 seconds in kilometers and 13.11 seconds in miles.

FAQ

Why is my marathon pace chart off by a few seconds?

The displayed pace has been rounded to whole seconds. Multiplying it across the course carries that rounding difference into the finish time.

Should I add up the pace printed on the chart?

Use the cumulative split column for your target. It keeps the original goal in the calculation, so rounding the displayed pace does not change the planned finish.

Does a negative error mean I will finish early?

It means the displayed steady pace would finish early over the stated distance. Your actual time depends on the distance you run, stops and changes in pace.
