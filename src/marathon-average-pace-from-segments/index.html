Marathon Average Pace From Unequal Segments

Run 30 km at 5:30/km and the last 12.195 km at 6:00/km: total time is 3:58:10.20, averaging 5:38.67/km. Calculate (30 × 330 + 12.195 × 360) ÷ 42.195.

Combine the first 30 km with the final segment

The first segment always takes 9,900 seconds, or 2:45:00. Multiply each final-segment pace by 12.195, add 9,900 seconds, and divide by 42.195 for the full-race average. Times below keep hundredths for verification.

Why does averaging the two pace labels give a different answer?

The midpoint of 5:30 and 6:00 is 5:45/km. That assigns equal weight to the two segments even though the first covers 30 km and the second 12.195 km. At 5:45/km over a full marathon, the finish would be 4:02:37.28, about 4:27.08 later than the worked example.

When can I average two pace labels directly?

For two equal distances, the arithmetic mean works. One kilometer at 5:30 plus one at 6:00 takes 11:30 across two kilometers, giving 5:45/km. Keep the distances in the same unit before combining them.

What if the segments last equal amounts of time?

Thirty minutes at 5:00/km covers 6 km. Thirty minutes at 6:00/km covers 5 km. The combined 60 minutes cover 11 km, so the average is 5:27.27/km. Equal time gives different distances, so divide the full 3,600 seconds by 11.
