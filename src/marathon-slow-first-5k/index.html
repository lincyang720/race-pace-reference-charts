Marathon Pace After a Slow First 5K

For a 4:00 marathon, losing 60 seconds against your planned first 5K split requires 1.61 seconds/km faster over the remaining 37.195 km. Divide 60 by 37.195. The remaining average becomes 5:39.66/km.

Spread the opening delay over the remaining distance

The exact four-hour pace is 14,400 ÷ 42.195 seconds per kilometer. The planned first 5K takes 28:26.36. Add your opening delay to that split, subtract from four hours, and divide by 37.195 km. The table uses unrounded planned times.

What if I try to recover a minute within the next kilometer?

Recovering 60 seconds in one kilometer requires that kilometer to be 60 seconds faster than planned. Spread over five kilometers, the arithmetic is 12 seconds per kilometer. Spread over the full 37.195 km, it is 1.61 seconds per kilometer. These are time comparisons, and the distance you choose changes the required adjustment.

What if the actual first 5K takes 30:00?

Four hours minus 30 minutes leaves 3:30:00, or 12,600 seconds. Dividing by 37.195 gives 5:38.76/km. Use the elapsed time from the start mat if your finish goal uses chip time. Include stopped time.

Must I keep the original finish goal?

You can also hold the original even pace. A one-minute delay followed by the original pace projects 4:01:00. The calculation shows the consequence of each choice. Use how you feel and the conditions to choose a sustainable plan.
